## Email 2 — Quick Start (Day 1)

**Subject:** Your first 5 minutes in Fabrica

**Preheader:** One real task, up and running before your coffee cools.

**Body:**

Five minutes is enough to go from login to your first run. Here's the shortest
path:

1. **Create a crew.** Pick a starter agent — a research agent, a writer, a
   developer. Start with one; you can add specialists after your first run.
2. **Write a one-paragraph brief.** Plain language is fine. Say what the outcome
   is, what to include, and where the source material lives.
3. **Review the plan, then approve the run.** Every agent starts with a plan
   before it executes. This is the checkpoint that keeps you in control.
4. **Read the output like a diff.** Review what came back before it moves
   anywhere. Bad result? Edit the brief and rerun. No babysitting, just review.

A tip from us: brief the agent the way you'd brief a new contractor — with
scope, constraints, and a definition of done. The clearer the brief, the fewer
reruns.

## Email 3 — Pro Tips (Day 3)

**Subject:** 3 ways to move 3x the work (no magic, just mechanics)

**Preheader:** Parallel crews, better briefs, and review that doesn't block.

**Body:**

You've done a first run. Now let's make Fabrica pay for itself — not with "10x"
promises, but with three concrete mechanics that compound:

**1. Run the crew in parallel, not in sequence.**
Fabrica's whole point is that one task shouldn't queue behind another. Put three
independent jobs in motion at once — research, outreach, a fix — each in its own
workspace so nothing collides. Your capacity scales with the crew, not your
typing.

**2. Reuse briefs like templates.**
A good brief is worth more than a prompt. Save the ones that produced strong
output, and rerun them with new source material. Founders stop re-explaining the
same work the day they start treating briefs as reusable assets.

**3. Move the approval gate, don't remove it.**
Consequential work should wait for you. But for low-risk, repeatable jobs —
summaries, drafts, routine analysis — move the gate so it only pings you when
the output is ready. That's not delegation without control; it's control at the
point where it matters.

One founder put it plainly: "The tools should carry the work. The founder should
keep the call." That's the operating principle.
