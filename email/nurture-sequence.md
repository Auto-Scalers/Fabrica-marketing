## Email 1 — Welcome (immediate)

**Send:** Immediately after Supabase signup confirmation.

**Subject:** Your Fabrica command center is ready

**Preview text:** Direct the crew. Keep the call. Here's where to start.

**Body:**

Welcome to Fabrica.

You now have one place to direct specialized agents across business and coding work — and to stay in control of what they do. Assign a clear brief, see what is running, review the plan, and approve the steps that matter.

Start simple. Pick one job that keeps landing on your plate — a research brief, a marketing draft, a code task — and give it to a single agent first. You do not need to wire up a full crew on day one.

Here is the loop that runs everything:

1. **Brief the agent** in plain language.
2. **Review the plan** before it runs.
3. **Approve the run** when it looks right.
4. **Review the output** and decide what moves next.

Your business, automated. Your judgment, still in the loop.

**Preview text:** Five minutes to a brief, a plan, and a running agent.

Yesterday you got the keys. Today, put the crew to work.

A first workflow takes about five minutes:

1. **Write the brief.** "Research our top three competitors' pricing and summarize changes this quarter." Plain language works.
2. **Pick the agent.** Start with one specialist — research, marketing, or development.
3. **Review the plan.** Fabrica will show you the steps before anything runs.
4. **Approve, then review.** The agent runs, and you get a clear output to check, edit, or rerun.

The point of the first run is not a perfect result. It is to feel the loop: brief, review, approve, review output. That loop is the whole system — everything else is a faster version of it.

If you hit a wall, reply to this email. A human on the Fabrica team will answer.

## Email 3 — Feature Spotlight: Parallel agents (Day 3)

**Send:** Three days after signup.

**Subject:** The trick that multiplies your capacity

**Preview text:** Several focused jobs in motion at once — each with its own brief and review point.

**Body:**

You are probably the bottleneck between a brief and a result. Fabrica's answer is not a bigger prompt — it is a crew.

Give several focused jobs their own brief and let them run in parallel. A researcher drafts market analysis while a developer starts a code task and a writer produces launch copy — each in its own space, each with visible status, each waiting at its review point when ready.

What does not change: every step that matters still comes back to you. Parallel work means more momentum, not less control.

Try this today:

1. Pick **two independent jobs** you have been deferring.
2. Brief one agent for each.
3. Approve both plans, then watch the status board.
4. Review the outputs in your own order — not the order they finished.

## Email 4 — Feature Spotlight: Approval gates (Day 5)

**Send:** Five days after signup.

**Subject:** Let work move. Keep the call with you.

**Preview text:** Approval gates keep consequential steps inside your rules.

**Body:**

Autonomy is useful. It is only safe when you choose where it stops.

Approval gates are the checkpoints that keep you in the loop: an agent runs until it reaches a decision point you flagged, then pauses and hands the plan, the diff, or the output back to you for a call. Approve to continue, or edit the brief and rerun.

That is what separates Fabrica from a black box. The work moves on its own, but authority stays where it belongs — with you.

Set a gate on a consequential step this week:

1. Open a workflow and find the step where a decision changes direction — a large spend, a code merge, a post going out.
2. Add an approval gate at that step.
3. Run it. Notice the difference between "let it run" and "let it run until I say so."

## Email 7 — Advanced Tips (Day 14)

**Send:** Fourteen days after signup.

**Subject:** Running the crew like a systems person

**Preview text:** Budgets, checkpoints, and the habits that separate idle experiments from real capacity.

**Body:**

Two weeks in, you have felt the loop. Here are the controls that make it repeatable at scale.

**Set budgets before you delegate.** Give an agent a spend limit up front. When it hits the cap, it stops — no surprise invoices, no opaque credit pools. Bring your own model keys and watch inference costs stay visible.

**Add checkpoints on long runs.** A single gate at the end is too late for multi-step work. Drop checkpoints between phases so a wrong turn costs you one step, not the whole run.

**Review the plan, not just the output.** The plan review is where you save the rerun. If the plan is wrong, fix the brief and rerun cheaply — before the agent burns time and budget heading in the wrong direction.

**Keep briefs reusable.** Write a brief once in plain language, then reuse it with a new date or scope. Your best workflows become templates that take minutes to redeploy.

That is the whole system: brief the crew, review the plan, approve the run, review the output. Fourteen days in, it should feel less like a tool and more like the way you work.

We will be here when you need to scale it further.

## Production notes

- **Trigger:** Supabase `auth.users` insert event → enqueue Email 1 immediately; schedule 2–7 on Day 1/3/5/7/10/14.
- **Personalization:** Merge tags for first name; reference the signup workflow where available.
- **Suppression:** Pause the sequence if the user has not logged in since Email 2; suppress Email 6 if the user has already submitted feedback or replied.
- **Brand check:** No blacklist terms ("AI-powered," "revolutionary," "game-changing"); no unverified metrics or testimonials. Single orange CTA per email. Use Forge Dark field with white/Steel Light body text; Geist for type.
- **Compliance:** Include unsubscribe link and physical sender address in every send. No fabricated quotes, counts, or case-study numbers.
